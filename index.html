Quoting engine architecture

A diagram of how one job moves through the engine. It shows structure and direction of flow only. It carries no counts, metrics or live state. Every node is drawn as the same geodesic sphere, sized by rank, because the system is made of the same object at every scale. Bronze lines are the job moving forward. Slate lines are data arriving. Sage lines are the cleared quote leaving and the calibration coming back.

The main path has five stations, read left to right on a desktop and top to bottom on a phone.

  1. Lead in. A request arrives, from a form, a call or a partner handoff.
  2. Scope sheet. The request becomes a structured job: what is being replaced, what is being added, what has to be reached.
  3. Pricing engine, at the centre and the largest node. It prices the scope sheet against supplier cost and the shop's own rates. Everything the engine reads arrives here.
  4. Margin gate. The priced job is held against the shop's floor. Nothing leaves under it.
  5. Quote out. The cleared quote is released to the customer, drawn on the same sage path as every other verified return.

Five live supplier feeds converge on the pricing engine from above. Each is a separate pull, and each is redrawn per quote rather than cached from a catalogue.

  • Price book
  • Stock level
  • Lead time
  • Freight
  • Tax rules

Three of the shop's own numbers come in from below. Supplier cost on its own is a list price, not a quote. These are what make the number theirs.

  • Rate card, into the pricing engine.
  • Labor hours, into the pricing engine.
  • Markup rules, into the margin gate, because that is the station the rules are tested at.

Priced job history is the wide arc across the lower field. Sixteen small nodes, placed by a golden-angle distribution rather than at random. It has three connections, each drawn once rather than once per node:

  • One trunk down from the margin gate: every quote that goes out lands in the field.
  • Lateral order: a single line running through every job in sequence, which is the arc itself.
  • One calibration return up into the pricing engine, which is what closes the loop. What the shop actually won at what margin feeds the next quote.
